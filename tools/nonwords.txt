# Spellings the word list holds that are not words.
#
# SCOWL 80 is generous on purpose — LEGAL is what a player may *guess*, and refusing an
# ordinary word reads as a bug rather than a rule. The price of that generosity is that it
# also carries the pieces English is built out of: bare affixes (`ing`, `ness`, `ation`),
# inflection fragments (`ed`, `ers`, `ted`), letter names and abbreviations (`rs`, `st`,
# `cf`), and combining forms that never stand alone (`abl`, `enc`, `thermo`). Every one of
# them is a legal *subword*, so every one of them is a move — and `walk + ing = walking` is
# the move nobody wants, because it is available from almost every word and says nothing
# about either end of it. `ing` alone carries one legal move in thirty-seven.
#
# So this is not a filter on taste. It says these spellings are not in the game's English,
# and the corpus is built without them: not a node, not a subword, not in the dictionary a
# typed guess is resolved against. One word per line, '#' starts a comment.
